Membrane repair · tissue regeneration

MG53 (TRIM72)

Also called Mitsugumin 53. First described by the Ma laboratory (Robert Wood Johnson Medical School, Rutgers University, 2009) as a component of the cell's intrinsic membrane-repair machinery.

The one-line version

MG53 is a molecular "first responder" that patches torn cell membranes.

Every cell membrane takes damage — from muscle contraction, oxidative stress, or injury. MG53 detects that damage, assembles into a repair structure at the wound site, and reseals the membrane before the cell dies. It belongs to the TRIM (tripartite motif) family of proteins, built from a RING domain, a B-box domain, a coiled-coil domain, and an MG53-specific C-terminal domain that carries out the membrane-repair function.

For the lab

MG53 senses phosphatidylserine (PS) exposed on the inner leaflet of a disrupted plasma membrane. PS binding triggers MG53 oligomerization and translocation to the injury site, where it nucleates a repair patch and recruits dysferlin, caveolin-3, and annexins to complete resealing. Zinc coordination through the B-box motif is required for oligomerization and for MG53's chaperone activity toward metabolic enzymes such as ADH and ALDH2.

In plain terms

Think of MG53 as a repair crew. When a cell's outer wall gets a hole in it, MG53 is the first to notice, rushes to the site, and builds a patch to seal it — like a self-sealing tire. Without that patch, the cell can leak and die.

Mechanism

Four steps from injury to resealed membrane

  1. 1

    Sensing. Membrane injury exposes phosphatidylserine on the cytosolic face of the plasma membrane; MG53 binds it selectively.

  2. 2

    Oligomerization & translocation. PS binding drives MG53 self-assembly and rapid movement from the cytosol to the wound site.

  3. 3

    Repair-patch formation. Assembled MG53 nucleates a membrane patch that physically covers the lesion, halting leakage of intracellular contents.

  4. 4

    Recruitment of repair machinery. MG53 recruits dysferlin, annexins, and caveolin-3 to stabilize and complete resealing, restoring membrane integrity.

Beyond membrane repair

Disease contexts under active investigation

Because nearly every tissue relies on membrane integrity, MG53 biology extends well past skeletal muscle. Current and recent lab work spans:

Muscular dystrophy & aging

Restoring membrane repair capacity in dystrophic and aging muscle; MG53 as a myokine for regeneration.

Cardioprotection

Limiting ischemia-reperfusion injury after myocardial infarction, cardiac surgery, and transplantation.

Acute lung injury

rhMG53 mitigates smoke-inhalation injury, warm ischemia in lung transplant, and nitrogen-mustard exposure.

Kidney injury

Protecting proximal tubular cells after contrast exposure, trauma, and traumatic-brain-injury-associated AKI.

ALS & neuromuscular disease

Sarcolemma repair deficits at the neuromuscular junction as a contributor to ALS progression.

Wound & corneal healing

Diabetic wound healing, hair-follicle regeneration, and corneal wound repair via sustained-release rhMG53.

Oncology

Nuclear MG53 suppresses KIF11 transcription and pancreatic tumor growth; anti-proliferative in colorectal carcinoma.

Inflammation & sepsis

MG53 restrains NLRP3 inflammasome activation in colitis and septic cardiac dysfunction via ATF2 ubiquitination.

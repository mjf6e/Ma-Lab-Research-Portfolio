Calcium signaling · triad-junction structure

MG29

Mitsugumin 29 — a small (107 amino-acid), synaptophysin-family protein first characterized by the Ma laboratory at the skeletal-muscle triad junction.

The one-line version

A structural protein that keeps calcium signaling organized — and, in other cell types, keeps division and death in balance.

MG29 has a compact, beta-strand-stabilized N-terminal domain and an intrinsically disordered, charge-rich C-terminal domain. In skeletal muscle, it helps organize the membrane architecture of the triad junction — the site where plasma-membrane and sarcoplasmic-reticulum calcium signaling machinery meet. Independently, MG29 has emerged as a transcriptional regulator implicated in cancer cell proliferation and survival.

For the lab

MG29 interacts with TRPC3/TRPC4 and the ryanodine receptor to support store-operated Ca²⁺ entry (SOCE) and to maintain triad-junction and t-tubule architecture; mg29-null muscle shows disrupted SOCE and increased fatigability. In non-muscle contexts, MG29 inhibits the transcription factor E2F1 (restraining cell-cycle progression) and binds the pro-apoptotic protein Bax, and is induced by cellular stress such as heat shock, oxidative stress, and DNA damage.

In plain terms

Picture MG29 as scaffolding that keeps a muscle cell's calcium-handling machinery in the right place, so the cell can respond quickly and fatigue less easily. Outside of muscle, the same protein appears to act like a brake — slowing down runaway cell division and helping cells survive stress, which is why it's of interest in cancer biology.

Functions under study

Two distinct roles, one protein

  1. 1

    Store-operated calcium entry. MG29 loss disrupts SOCE and t-tubule/triad architecture, increasing fatigue susceptibility in skeletal muscle and altering t-tubule structure in the failing heart.

  2. 2

    Cell-cycle regulation. MG29 inhibits E2F1 activity, restraining expression of genes that drive cell-cycle progression.

  3. 3

    Apoptosis modulation. By binding Bax, MG29 can limit premature apoptotic cell death.

  4. 4

    Stress response. MG29 expression rises with heat shock, oxidative stress, and DNA damage, consistent with a role in cellular stress adaptation.

Disease relevance

From muscle aging to cancer biology

Muscle aging

MG29 expression declines with age alongside compromised SOCE and Ca²⁺ homeostasis, correlating with sarcopenia.

Heart failure

Cardiac induction of MG29 partially restores t-tubule structure and function in a dilated-cardiomyopathy model.

Oncology

MG29 is dysregulated in breast, prostate, and lung cancer; overexpression associates with proliferation and apoptosis resistance, making it a candidate therapeutic target.

Immune resolution · inflammation biomarker & target

Citrullinated Histone H3 (CitH3) & NETosis

NETosis is the process by which neutrophils release web-like extracellular traps (NETs) to catch pathogens. CitH3 is both a driver of that process and a measurable biomarker of it.

The one-line version

A defense mechanism that, left unchecked, becomes the injury itself.

NETosis lets neutrophils trap and kill bacteria, fungi, and viruses by expelling decondensed chromatin studded with antimicrobial proteins. Citrullination of histone H3 — catalyzed by peptidylarginine deiminases PAD2/PAD4 — is the step that loosens chromatin enough for this to happen. The same CitH3 that escapes the cell then acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP), amplifying inflammation in tissue far from the original infection.

For the lab

PAD2/PAD4 convert arginine residues on histone H3 (R2, R8, R17, R26) to citrulline, neutralizing the tails' positive charge and weakening histone–DNA interaction. This drives chromatin decondensation, nuclear envelope rupture, and extrusion of NETs. Extracellular CitH3 subsequently activates TLR2 on macrophages, triggering a Ca²⁺-dependent PAD2 auto-citrullination feedback loop that sustains inflammation independent of ongoing infection.

In plain terms

Imagine a white blood cell throwing a net made of its own DNA to catch germs. CitH3 is the "unspooling" signal that lets the DNA loosen enough to be thrown. The problem is that once that net is out, it keeps signaling "danger" to the rest of the body — even after the germs are gone.

Mechanism

From citrullination to circulating biomarker

  1. 1

    Citrullination. PAD2/PAD4 convert histone H3 arginines to citrulline, stripping the chromatin's positive charge.

  2. 2

    Chromatin decondensation. Weakened histone–DNA binding lets tightly packed chromatin unfold and expand.

  3. 3

    Nuclear & plasma membrane rupture. Decondensed chromatin mixes with granular antimicrobial proteins and is expelled as a NET.

  4. 4

    Systemic signaling. Circulating CitH3 acts as a DAMP, activating TLR2 on macrophages and sustaining inflammation and coagulation beyond the original site.

Disease relevance

CitH3 as biomarker and drug target

A humanized anti-CitH3 monoclonal antibody (hCitH3-mAb) developed with the lab's collaborators is in active preclinical testing across several of these contexts.

Sepsis

CitH3 drives macrophage dysfunction via TLR2/PAD2; hCitH3-mAb improves survival in LPS and P. aeruginosa models.

Acute lung injury

Reduces neutrophil infiltration and NLRP3 activation in smoke-inhalation and sepsis-associated ALI models.

Cardiac ischemia-reperfusion

Neutralizing CitH3 shrinks infarct size and preserves ejection fraction after myocardial infarction.

Autoimmune disease

CitH3 in NETs may act as an autoantigen, implicated in lupus and rheumatoid arthritis pathogenesis.

Thrombotic disease

NET-associated CitH3 contributes to pathological clot formation, a target for anticoagulant strategies.

Cancer

CitH3-driven NETosis has been linked to tumor progression and metastasis in several malignancies.

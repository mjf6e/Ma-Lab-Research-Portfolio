Principal Investigator

Jianjie Ma, PhD

William H. Muller Endowed Professor of Thoracic Surgery · Director, Division of Surgical Sciences · Department of Surgery, University of Virginia School of Medicine

From physics to physiology

Forty years at the bench, three discoveries deep

Dr. Ma trained in physics (B.S., Wuhan University, 1983) before moving into physiology and biophysics (Ph.D., Baylor College of Medicine, 1989). His early work on calcium signaling and excitation–contraction coupling contributed to identifying the ryanodine receptor as the sarcoplasmic reticulum's calcium-release channel and to discovering store-operated calcium entry in skeletal muscle — the line of inquiry that later produced MG29. That same immuno-proteomic approach to the muscle triad junction, developed over three decades with longtime collaborator Dr. Hiroshi Takeshima, led in 2009 to the discovery of MG53 as a core component of the cell's membrane-repair machinery. Most recently, the lab's work on NETosis has produced hCitH3-mAb, a first-in-class humanized antibody now authorized by the FDA for first-in-human studies.

Academic path

  • 2022–present — William H. Muller Endowed Professor of Thoracic Surgery; Director, Division of Surgical Sciences, University of Virginia
  • 2012–2022 — Karl P. Klassen Chair of Thoracic Surgery, The Ohio State University; Vice Chair of Research, Dept. of Surgery (2019–2022)
  • 2001–2012 — University-named Professor, Robert Wood Johnson Medical School / Rutgers University; Founder & Director, Graduate Program in Physiology and Integrative Biology
  • 1996–2001 — Associate Professor with tenure, Case Western Reserve University
  • 1989–1992 — Instructor, then Assistant Professor of Physiology, Rush University School of Medicine

In his own words

On mentorship and team science

"My mentoring philosophy is rooted in the belief that scientific achievement is most meaningful when it creates opportunities for others to succeed. The growth and accomplishments of my trainees will always represent my most enduring legacy." Dr. Ma's leadership of the Division of Surgical Sciences emphasizes partnership over hierarchy — bringing surgeons, physician-scientists, engineers, basic scientists, and industry partners together around shared problems.

Skeletal muscle · aging

MG53 function in muscle aging

Elderly individuals have a reduced capacity to repair injured muscle, contributing to muscle weakness and loss of physical activity. This project uses biochemistry, genome editing, and in vivo models to understand diminished MG53 myokine function in aging muscle, and tests whether inducible MG53 secretion can rejuvenate muscle satellite cells to enhance repair and regeneration.

Neurodegeneration

Mitigating neuroinflammation and enhancing neuronal integrity in Alzheimer's disease

There is currently no treatment that prevents or reverses cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease. This project examines MG53's dual role in tissue repair and anti-inflammatory signaling as a novel route to neuroprotection and mitigating neurodegeneration.

Infectious disease · lung & heart

Protein therapy to treat virus-induced cardiopulmonary injury

Influenza and other aggressive viral infections recur at epidemic scale every year. This project examines MG53's combined tissue-repair and anti-inflammatory function as a strategy to mitigate virus-induced injury across the heart and lungs.

Neuromuscular disease

Multifaceted role of MG53 in alleviating neuromuscular function decline in ALS

ALS is an incurable, fatal disease driven by degeneration of muscle cells and the nerves that control them. This project combines imaging and functional assays to study the balance between membrane damage and repair at the neuromuscular junction in ALS patient and mouse-model tissue, and tests whether boosting muscle membrane repair can slow disease progression.

Ophthalmology · transplantation

Targeting the multicellular process of corneal regeneration and vascularization to enhance the outcome of corneal transplantation

$3.4M · NIH award, announced 2024

Corneal transplants in high-risk, vascularized or inflamed host beds fail at substantially higher rates. This project develops MG53 as a protein therapeutic to reduce inflammation and vascularization while promoting regeneration during corneal graft healing, in collaboration with investigators at Chapman University and The Ohio State University.

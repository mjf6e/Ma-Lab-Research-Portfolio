Thirteen active federal awards, spanning R01, U01, R41, K08, DoD, and T32 mechanisms. Project numbers below can be looked up directly on NIH RePORTER ↗ for budget and abstract detail.

As Principal Investigator

R01 / U01 / R41 awards

R01AG071676 · PI · 2022–2027

MG53 function in muscle aging

Elderly individuals have a reduced capacity to repair injured muscle, contributing to weakness and loss of physical activity. This project examines diminished MG53 myokine function in aging muscle and tests whether inducible MG53 secretion can rejuvenate muscle satellite cells to enhance repair and regeneration.

U01EY032973 · PI · 2022–2027

First Aid Medicine to Treat Vesicant-Induced Corneal Injury

Develops rhMG53-based countermeasures for corneal injury caused by vesicant (chemical) exposure, extending the lab's work on nitrogen-mustard-induced skin and lung injury to the eye.

R01AG072430 · PI · 2022–2027

Mitigating neuroinflammation and enhancing neuronal integrity in Alzheimer's disease

There is currently no treatment that prevents or reverses cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease. This project examines MG53's dual role in tissue repair and anti-inflammatory signaling as a route to neuroprotection.

R01EY036243 · PI · 2024–2029

Targeting the multicellular process of corneal regeneration and vascularization to enhance outcome of cornea transplantation

$3.4M · announced 2024

Corneal transplants in high-risk, vascularized or inflamed host beds fail at substantially higher rates. This project develops MG53 as a protein therapeutic to reduce inflammation and vascularization while promoting regeneration during graft healing, with collaborators at Chapman University and The Ohio State University. Source: UVA Department of Surgery ↗

R01HL157215 · PI · 2022–2026

Protein therapy to treat virus-induced cardiopulmonary injury

Examines MG53's combined tissue-repair and anti-inflammatory function as a strategy to mitigate influenza- and other virus-induced injury across heart and lungs.

R41DK142247 · PI · 2025–2026

Controlling NETosis to Treat Diabetic Wounds

An SBIR/STTR-track project applying the lab's NETosis and CitH3 biology to chronic, poorly healing diabetic wounds.

Multifaceted role of MG53 in alleviating neuromuscular function decline in ALS

Studies the balance between membrane damage and repair at the neuromuscular junction in ALS patient and mouse-model tissue, testing whether boosting muscle membrane repair can slow disease progression.

Milestone

hCitH3-mAb reaches first-in-human studies

The lab's humanized anti-CitH3 monoclonal antibody has received FDA authorization for first-in-human clinical studies, following preclinical results showing reduced cytokine production, mortality, and acute lung injury in sepsis models. UVA has filed a patent application related to this work.
